# Game direction

Make every lap feel like a sequence of opportunities: a drift to charge a boost, an item to reverse a battle, a leap into a different landscape, or a risky shortcut across grass. Driving must be enjoyable before items are added.

## Pillars

- Fast and responsive: predictable steering, satisfying drift control, generous recovery, and clear boost feedback.
- Spectacular but readable: long jumps, changing elevation, moving set pieces, and dramatic scenery while preserving a visible route and landing zone.
- Playful rivalry: original offensive, defensive, and movement items; visible counterplay and short interruptions.
- Varied routes: surfaces, shortcuts, banking, obstacles, and alternate lines that reward learning the course.
- Polished presentation: appealing kart/driver silhouettes, cohesive materials, responsive camera, layered audio, and effects that communicate state.

## First course: Canopy Rush

A coastal starting grid climbs into lush cliffside forest, spirals around an overgrown waterworks, and launches across a gorge into sunlit ruins before returning to the beach. This is a concept; no course art exists yet.

Target a 60–90 second lap: opening drift, grass shortcut with a speed cost, first item row, a telegraphed rotating gate, a banked climb, a launch pad with a wide landing, and a final overtaking straight. Include a safe route alongside the risky shortcut. Start with one large jump and one moving hazard, then tune their spacing with playtests.

## Later course themes

| Course concept | Scenery / signature | Gameplay variation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Furnace Freight | Foundry, mine, lava-lit cavern | Timed crushers, moving freight, conveyors |
| Cloudbreak Circuit | Floating gardens and storm observatory | Huge launch chains, wind zones, branching landings |
| Midnight Market | Neon harbor, rooftops, canals | Crossing traffic, narrow shortcuts, wet surface sections |

These are backlog concepts, not parallel production commitments. Share road kits, prop systems, and interaction components while keeping each course's silhouette, palette, and signature distinct.

## First item set

Start with a short boost, a forward projectile, a dropped hazard, and a shield. All need recognizable silhouettes, sound cues, owner protection rules, expiry, and pooling. Expand to position-weighted selection after race positions are trustworthy. Avoid long stuns or repeated unavoidable hits.

## Initial quality target

Aim for stable 60 fps at 1080p on a named baseline Mac and Windows machine; nominate and measure those devices during the first playable milestone. Do not promise performance before profiling. Target a solo race against seven AI racers after time-trial handling works.

The confirmed destination is a DIY Wii-style console: the computer runs and displays the game while phones act as controllers. Prove one phone controlling one kart early. The eventual local party mode needs shared-screen or split-screen design; prototype two-player split-screen and profile four players before promising that count. Internet multiplayer is a separate feature, not required for phones controlling a single local game.
